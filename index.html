00 — The Lens · July 2026

Most brands don't have an awareness problem. They have a self‑awareness problem.

When every organisation rents the same AI, intelligence stops being an edge. What's left is what always distinguished the leaders you rated most — not the cleverest, but the ones who knew how they actually landed. That's emotional intelligence. When it belongs to an organisation rather than a person, it has a different name. Trust.

And AI is making it worse before it makes it better: it polishes what an organisation says without changing what stakeholders experience. The say–do gap widens. Edelman's 2026 barometer put it plainly — trust has moved from "how I feel about a brand" to "how I think a brand feels about me."

What's new is that it's now measurable — a capability, not a vibe. Echo Research puts organisational trust at £841bn — 28% of the FTSE 350's market cap. EQ, at business scale, is a line on the balance sheet.

01 — The Questions

Every leadership team is asking the same six questions. The AI age just made them urgent.

Twenty years of briefs distil to these. Each answered with the work that proves it — and the brands themselves, linked, so you can see who they became.

Q1Purpose & positioning
Who are we — now that AI can imitate anyone?

Style can be synthesised; substance can't. When any competitor can sound like you by Tuesday, the only defensible position is the one you actually hold. I find the truth a business already owns and build on that — so imitation flatters instead of threatens. Exxaro's "Powering possibility" has held for twenty years because it was never copy. It was a commitment. Frontline AIDS earned Gold because "JOIN US. END IT." was already true of them.

Q2Strategic narrative
Everyone has a story now. Why does nobody believe ours?

Generative tools made storytelling free — which made belief expensive. The work isn't a better story; it's closing the distance between the story and the experience so the narrative survives contact with reality. ICIS brought me back five years after the first positioning to plan the next chapter. Narrative that holds is narrative that was true.

Q4M&A & merger identity
We've just merged. Whose truth wins?

Neither — a merger that keeps score becomes two companies wearing one logo. Adenza worked because the position was built from what Calypso and AxiomSL could only claim together — "Platform to opportunity" — and the evidence picked the words. Nasdaq paid $10.5bn for the result.

Q5Proof & measurement
The board asks whether brand is working. What do we show them?

Not sentiment. Not awareness. Trust — measured as Clarity, Connection and Confidence, tracked like the leading indicator it is. Echo Research values organisational trust at £841bn across the FTSE 350; the honest question is your share of it. This is the discipline I've now productised as TrustOS — designed in the field, including a tracker built and deployed for Solmax.

Q6The AI practice
How do we use AI without losing ourselves?

Treat it as augmentation, not replacement — and say so out loud. I co-authored BCS's "AI and the Future of Work" with Geeks CEO Somayeh Aghnia: the case for an augmented-intelligence economy where machines scale the work and humans keep the judgement. It's how my own practice runs — AI-accelerated research and drafting, twenty years of pattern recognition doing the deciding, and a colophon on everything declaring which was which. Self-awareness starts at home.
