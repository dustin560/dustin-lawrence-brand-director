00 — The Lens · July 2026

Most brands don't have an awareness problem.
They have a self-awareness problem.

When every organisation rents the same AI, intelligence stops being an edge. What's left is what always distinguished the leaders you rated most — not the cleverest, but the ones who knew how they actually landed. That's emotional intelligence. When it belongs to an organisation rather than a person, it has a different name. Trust.

And AI is making it worse before it makes it better: it polishes what an organisation says without changing what stakeholders experience. The say–do gap widens. Edelman's 2026 barometer put it plainly — trust has moved from "how I feel about a brand" to "how I think a brand feels about me." That is not an awareness question. It's a self-awareness question.

Nothing here is new. Aristotle knew. Drucker knew. What's new is that it's now measurable — which finally makes it a capability, not a vibe. And that Echo Research puts organisational trust at £841bn — 28% of the FTSE 350's market cap. EQ, at business scale, is a line on the balance sheet.

Logical.

Story without structure is just noise. I work in evidence — interviews, audits, data — then translate the mess into a single-minded idea both leadership and the market can stand on.

Creative.

The best strategy still has to make someone want to lean in. I guide identity, voice, naming and architecture so the idea doesn't die in the deck — it lives in the room.
